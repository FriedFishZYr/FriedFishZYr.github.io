Case study · KeyPalette

2026 · Product design + engineering

K E Y P A L E T T E

Design a keycap colorway before you buy it.

KeyPalette is a free, browser-only planner for mechanical keyboard enthusiasts. Pick a layout, paint keys by zone, pull a palette from a photo, and share the result as a link. No account, no install, nothing uploaded.

01Overview

01

One job, and no account standing between you and it.

The brief

Keycap sets are bought months before they ship, from renders. Enthusiasts plan colorways in spreadsheets, Reddit threads and vendor-locked 3D configurators. There was no simple, layout-agnostic canvas where you could try a palette on your own board in thirty seconds.

What shipped

An interactive SVG keyboard with 13 layouts, zone and same-color selection, palette extraction from photos, simulated keycap profiles, undo history, five local designs, side-by-side comparison, and PNG, SVG, JSON and share-link exports. Everything lives in the browser; the share link is the whole design.

  • 13

    layouts

    ANSI + ISO, HHKB, 96%, Planck

  • 16

    presets

    zone-based, apply to any layout

  • 0

    bytes uploaded

    no server, no analytics, no cookies

  • 300 KB

    bundle

    down from 473 KB after dropping html2canvas

02The problem

02

Existing tools were built for vendors, not for the person holding a photo.

Keyboard enthusiasts plan colorways from renders, months before a set ships. The planning itself happened in spreadsheets, Reddit threads and whatever the vendor offered.

  • Too complex

    Full 3D configurators are tied to one vendor’s product line, need an account to save, and take minutes to load for a thirty-second question.

  • Too limited

    Static swatch grids show colors, not a board. No layout, no zones, no sense of how an accent set reads next to the alphas.

  • Missing entirely

    “I saw this palette on Reddit. How would it look on my 65%?” had no answer that did not involve Photoshop.

Who it is for

  • Hobbyists planning a custom keycap set
  • Designers exploring colorway ideas
  • Buyers previewing before ordering from KBDfans, NovelKeys or Drop

03Principles

03

Four rules that settled most of the arguments.

  1. 01

    The keyboard is the canvas.

    Every control steps back. The board stays centered and scales to fit the viewport; the toolbar scrolls on its own so the thing you are designing never leaves the screen.

  2. 02

    Nothing leaves the browser.

    Designs live in localStorage. A share link carries the whole design in the URL hash, deflated to about 600 characters for a TKL. There is no server to trust, so there is no privacy policy to read.

  3. 03

    Think in zones, not keys.

    Alphas, modifiers, arrows, navigation, numpad. Presets and selections work per zone, which is why one preset applies cleanly to all 13 layouts and why Alt-click can grab every key sharing a color.

  4. 04

    Depth is opt-in.

    Flat is the default render: clean, fast, honest. SA, OEM, Cherry and DSA shading are one click away for people who want to see how a sculpted set catches light, but nobody is forced through it.

04Process

04

Three releases, each one shippable on its own.

Scope was cut by tier, not by feature list. V1 had to be a complete product; V2 and V3 layered depth on top without rewriting the state shape.

  1. V1

    April 2026

    MVP

    Layout selector, interactive SVG board, per-key and zone color, case color and finish, localStorage with five named slots, light and dark workspace.

  2. V2

    May 2026

    Enhanced UX

    Palette extraction from a photo, eight presets, PNG export with a color legend, JSON import and export, color history strip, click-to-copy hex badge.

  3. V3

    June 2026

    Visual fidelity

    Keycap profile shading, auto-contrast legends, comparison mode, scale-to-fit app shell, frame-ring selection, sixteen presets.

  4. 3.1

    Sep – Oct 2026

    Launch readiness

    Share links, ISO, HHKB, 96% and Planck layouts, undo and redo, SVG export, keyboard navigation, storage safety, 46 tests, CI, GitHub Pages, mobile UI.

→The commit log is the honest version of this timeline: the V3 step for keycap realism was pushed with the message “looks bad though”. Three months later the renderer was rewritten so walls are always darker than the top, the front face is lit separately, and every cap casts a contact shadow.

05Interface

05

The board in the middle, everything else in the margins.

A fixed-height shell. The toolbar scrolls on its own; the keyboard stays centered and shrinks to fit, never enlarged past 1×. On narrow screens the editor becomes a sheet under the board.

Narrow screens

Board first, controls second.

On phones the toolbar becomes a bottom sheet with a drag handle, the header actions collapse into a menu, and the board keeps a tappable minimum size with a Zoom control. The design flow stays identical: pick a group, pick a color, share.

06Decisions

06

Six calls worth explaining.

Most of these reversed something the original spec asked for. Each one is logged in the changelog with the reason.

  1. 01

    Selection is a frame, not a fill.

    V1 and V2 tinted selected keys with a white overlay, so a key never showed its true color while you were editing it. V3 decoupled feedback from fill: a 2px accent ring with a contrasting halo, a faint ring on hover, a geometric press. The fill only ever shows the real color.

    Trade-offCosts a little subtlety on very light caps; worth it for honesty.

  2. 02

    A palette, not an eyedropper.

    The roadmap called for a point-sample cursor over an uploaded photo. Extracting six to eight dominant colors as swatches was simpler to build and more useful in practice: people want the photo’s palette, not one pixel of it.

    Trade-offThe point-sample cursor stays on the list for people who do want one pixel.

  3. 03

    Flat by default.

    The V3 spec said the Cherry profile should ship as the default. Flat won: it reads faster, it has no shading artifacts on black caps, and the sculpted look is one click away for anyone who wants it.

    Trade-offScreenshots look less “real” at first glance. The export is the truth anyway.

  4. 04

    The share link is the database.

    Instead of a backend, the design is serialized, deflated and written into the URL hash: about 600 characters for a TKL. Opening a link adds a new saved design, or replaces the current one undoably when all five slots are full.

    Trade-offLong URLs. Designs on a new domain do not follow you, so the About dialog offers a backup download.

  5. 05

    Drop html2canvas.

    DOM capture was roughly 200 KB of unmaintained code and produced soft output. The board is already an SVG, so the exporter rasterizes it at 2× and paints the legend onto a canvas. The production bundle went from 473 KB to 300 KB.

    Trade-offLegend layout had to be reimplemented by hand, and is now covered by tests.

  6. 06

    Never destroy data you cannot read.

    Unreadable saved data is moved to a timestamped backup key and offered for download instead of being overwritten; invalid fields are repaired one at a time. The error boundary and every error screen offer the same backup.

    Trade-offMore code paths to test. The alternative was silently losing someone’s five designs.

07Craft

07

Details that took longer than the features.

  • Keycap rendering

    Walls are always darker than the top. The front face is lit separately, the top is lifted slightly so black caps still show depth, sculpted profiles show more front face toward the back rows, each cap has a contact shadow, and a darker plate shows between keys.

  • Legends that stay readable

    Legend color is computed per key from the cap’s luminance: white on dark, black on light. A fixed custom color and top or front placement are optional. Legends moved out of CSS into SVG attributes so the export matches the screen.

  • Keyboard first

    One tab stop for the whole board. Arrow keys move between keys, Home and End jump to row ends, Enter or Space selects, Shift adds. Dialogs are native <dialog> elements with focus return and Escape handling even where browsers skip the cancel event.

  • Layouts that share ids

    ISO boards are derived from their ANSI counterparts by a function, not copied. HHKB, 96% and Planck are hand-built. A test validates every layout for overlapping keys and consistent ids, so a JSON file made on one board opens on another.

08Outcomes

08

Live, tested, and still a side project.

Where it stands

  • Public at friedfishzyr.github.io/KeyPalette since 24 October 2026
  • Every push to main runs lint, 46 tests and the build before deploying
  • 300 KB production bundle, three runtime dependencies, MIT license
  • Works offline after first load; nothing is sent anywhere, ever

What is next

  • KLE import for arbitrary boards and rotated layouts such as Alice
  • Per-key and per-zone legend colors for double-shot simulation
  • A community gallery and vendor color matching, both of which need a backend
  • Analytics, deliberately left out of the launch
